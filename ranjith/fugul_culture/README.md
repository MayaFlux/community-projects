# Fugul Culture

A colony of strange, filamented beings drifts and changes shape alongside a resonant musical web. Six musical voices move through a shared melodic contour, while the organisms answer the notes with changes of form. Neither the image nor the sound is merely an illustration of the other: they grow together as one evolving piece.

## The colony

Twenty-four organisms gather in a loose, overlapping cloud. Their shapes suggest six kinds of microscopic life: spined spores, many-armed forms, bristling spheres, soft bells with trailing tendrils, radiating shells, and elongated phages.

Each is drawn from a continuous tangle of fine lines. Bodies pulse and turn; ripples travel along their limbs; and their silhouettes slowly alter as limbs stretch, curl, flatten, recede, and grow again. The whole colony drifts as it changes, dense near its centre and open around its edges.

## The music

The sound moves through a web of thirty-two resonant strands. Six independent walkers explore its pitches in overlapping patterns, making a shifting fugue of pure tones, short clicks, and pulse trains. There is no added noise: each sound is a strike that rings out through the web.

The voices enter one by one, twelve seconds apart. Their shared contour keeps returning in different shapes (as an answer, a reversal, an inversion, or a doubled figure) while the pace gradually quickens. Two voices weave complementary rhythms together, and the notes slowly beat between the two stereo channels.

## One living composition

When heard and seen together, each organism lends its strand a character. Its form affects the colour and decay of the sound and the subtle beating between the ears. In turn, the musical voices leave traces in the colony: repeated strikes encourage the corresponding organism to evolve, with the musical figure guiding the direction of its change.

As more of the colony changes, the music's pace shifts slightly too. The image and sound remain distinct, but they continually influence one another.

## Running it

Include `src/Culture.hpp` and call `Culture::piece()` from `user_project.hpp` to experience the joined work. You can also call `Culture::organisms()` for the evolving image alone, or `Culture::culture()` for the music alone.

The visual work opens its own window and uses the usual fly camera controls. For the complete sound experience, use stereo output.
